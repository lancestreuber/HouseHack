# Stakeholders: what they say they need

**[V]** = we read the source (some sources are third-party summaries, e.g. citizenportal.ai). **[S]** = search snippet only.

## City of Pittsburgh
- **Housing Needs Assessment (Jan 2022) [S].** It is being implemented as zoning text amendments:
  - Minimum lot size change (done, May 2025).
  - Bill 2025-1545 (ADUs, parking, affordable-housing bonus). Council hearing held 9/23/2026, vote pending.
- **EO 2026-01 [S] and the March 2026 permitting plan [V].**
  - Triage by project size and complexity.
  - Better pre-application meetings.
  - The City will run public-input meetings in place of the Registered Community Organizations (RCOs).
  - "Investigating AI technologies to review applications for missing info." No vendor named, and no RFP or RFI found.
  - Phase II is a full zoning rewrite tied to the 2050 comprehensive plan (about $6M).
  - We found no housing production target or site-inventory commitment in the plan.
- **Results so far [V]** ([PublicSource 9/23/26](https://www.publicsource.org/pittsburgh-building-permits-faster-under-oconnor/)): Building and Development Application reviews went from 27 to 11 days. The obstacles still named are zoning, variances, and conflicts with the building code.
- **Framing from the Mayor's office [V]:** "laser-focus on growth."

## Pittsburgh Land Bank
- **Jan 2026 task force [V]:** about 270 condemned buildings and about 5,000 vacant lots in the tax-delinquent inventory. It asked for $32M a year for 5 years. Federal funding ends after 2027.
- **Sheriff-sale authority, April 2026 [V]:** about 9 months to a clear title, versus about 2 years before. A residential rehab pilot is expanding; applicants need proof of funds and a plan.
- **Acquisitions [S]:** mostly acquires where a buyer is already identified.

## Allegheny County
- **Innamorato's executive order, Feb 2026 [V]:**
  - The County's first housing needs assessment.
  - A land-bank review.
  - An affordable housing fund.
  - Incentives for municipal zoning reform.
- **Model ordinances [V]:** a library of zoning amendments and model ordinances for municipalities, delivered through the InterCOG Council.
- **Preservation-first framing [V]:** 44,000+ apartments renting under $1,000 a month were lost between 2019 and 2024. "We can build all we want, but if we continue to lose units…"
- **Unresolved:** the organizer data catalog lists an existing "Allegheny County Housing Needs Assessment," which conflicts with the "first" in the Feb 2026 order. Needs a check.

## State (DCED, PHFA)
- **PA Housing Action Plan 2026–2035 [V, summary page only]:**
  - Needs 450k units by 2035, with a projected shortfall of about 185k.
  - Modernize regulations and the Municipalities Planning Code.
  - Data sharing, repair of older stock, land banks, a one-stop shop.
  - No explicit site-scoring recommendation found.
- **PHARE 2026 [S]:** $95M+, prioritizing preservation or new affordable rental.
- **PHFA 2026 policy fellow [V]:** Dr. Evaine Sing is researching the roughly 27,000 vacant lots in Pittsburgh and Wilkinsburg, "many publicly owned and irregularly shaped." This is the closest match to Track 1 we found, and a possible person to validate with.

## Practitioners
- **ACTION-Housing, CEO Lena Andrews [V]:**
  - Rehab often costs more per square foot than new construction.
  - "Not a crisis we can solve with subsidy alone."
  - Wants zoning reform plus predictable permitting.
- **Rising Tide / Hazelwood Initiative, 2022 [V]:** the bottleneck was acquisition and title (2+ years to clear title, outbid at treasurer sale, cost of conservatorship). Buildings deteriorated while they waited. This predates the Land Bank's sheriff-sale change.
- **Inclusionary zoning cost [S]:** a 196-unit Uptown project was paused over it, and Walnut Capital sought an exemption.
- **Sept 2025 hearing on Bill 2025-1545 [V]:** CDCs (Lawrenceville United, OPDC) supported inclusionary zoning. Pro-Housing Pittsburgh and the building trades opposed parts of it.

## Assumptions this challenges (for discussion)
1. **"Which site is easiest" is not the bottleneck anyone names.** They name acquisition and title, the financing, subsidy or appraisal gap, preservation, inclusionary zoning cost, zoning and variances, and Land Bank funding.
2. **Nonprofits often work parcels they already control or target.** A citywide ranking may matter less to them than to the City or Land Bank.
3. **Permitting is improving fast.** Zoning and variances are the remaining regulatory friction, and the code is being rewritten.
4. **City and County want different things:** growth versus preservation, and one city versus 130 municipalities.
5. **Rehab versus new construction is central, not an edge case.**
6. **The only AI use the City has named is completeness checking.**

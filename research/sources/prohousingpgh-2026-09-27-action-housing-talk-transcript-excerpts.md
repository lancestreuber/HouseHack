# "Building Affordable Housing by Action Housing" (Pro-Housing Pittsburgh general meeting): transcript excerpts

- **Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vJ0ReB26gVA (71 min; uploaded 2024-04-16; event held 2024-04-11 per https://www.prohousingpgh.org/events/speaker-04-11-2024)
- **Speaker:** ACTION-Housing's Senior Development Officer (cited by role)
- **Retrieved:** 2026-09-27. Text is YouTube's **auto-generated (ASR) English captions**, pulled with the `youtube-transcript-api` Python package. It was not human-corrected. **The slides were not visible to us**, so dollar figures shown only on slides are missing.
- **Why saved:** it is the only first-hand local account we hold of how a Pittsburgh LIHTC deal is capitalized. Captions can disappear.

Timestamps are mm:ss. Quotes are ASR text with filler words ("um", "uh") removed. Square brackets are ours.

- [02:00] LIHTC is "how we fund a majority of the projects that we at ACTION-Housing do".
- [36:20] "construction costs, or as we call them hard cost versus soft cost … is split roughly 70/30".
- [36:40–37:50] Budget lines named: construction; land purchase; professional fees ("architectural, engineering, environmental"); syndication expenses; construction loan fees and interest ("way higher now"); lease-up cost; reserves, "a really big number … dictated by the Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency … rent reserve … operating reserves … a pretty big number you wouldn't see on maybe a market deal"; developer fee and overhead.
- [37:56] "about a seven-year project from beginning to end, most of it in about a three-year period".
- [38:50–39:50] Versus market rate: construction cost per square foot is "usually pretty close"; the differences are syndication fees, reserves, environmental requirements, and funding-source strings. Example: RACP, "a state grant program … requires … state prevailing wage … purchase requirements like for American steel … prevailing wage in particular is like a quick, you know, 20% maybe on your labor".
- [41:00] Project size: "somewhere around 50,000 square feet … maybe a little bit less" (speaker unsure).
- [42:00] "This is 100% affordable … everything is affordable at 60% area median income and below."
- [44:15–44:50] Timeline: preparing a competitive LIHTC application "might take let's say six months, then it may take another six months before they announce awards, and then it's about a year to do all your architectural, engineering … so you're already into it for … two years almost, and then construction timelines … now they're at 16 to 18 months".
- [45:30–46:05] Sources: "our projects, because they're becoming more expensive … and the tax credit equity is roughly staying the same, means we have to go find more funding sources … our newest record is 23 sources".
- [46:10] Unit mix: "35 units … 31 one bedrooms, three two bedrooms and one three bedroom".
- [47:00–48:00] Sources on this deal: tax credit equity; the URA; PHFA ("different pots of money"); FHLB Pittsburgh Affordable Housing Program (AHP).
- [48:20–49:05] "big cost overruns due to … unforeseen environmental issues, as well as COVID … we put in a sponsor loan … we put in almost [a] million dollars of our own money and then we deferred our developer fee … about 1.5 million … it's basically all plugged back into the project". Also: "these are not really loans that ever get paid back … soft loans".
- [50:30–51:30] Cash-flow waterfall: soft lenders "may take 50% of cash flow, they may take 25, it may take all of it, but if you don't have any cash flow at the end of the year you don't have to pay".
- [52:20–52:50] HUD rent comparability study: difference in annual rent between the affordable rents and market rents for the same building "$580,000 … that's the money that typically would go towards debt service".
- [53:35–54:00] PA credit allocation "$2.75 … per person"; LIHTC serves "60% AMI or less … in Pittsburgh the 2023 numbers were 42,000 for a single person, roughly 60,000 for a family of four".
- [55:25–56:30] "total allocation for 2023 of 35 and a half million … 73 applications … only 33 awards statewide … City of Pittsburgh got six awards, which is a lot, typically we only get three … two more in the county for a total of eight … 361 units but 320 of those … are for low income".
- [57:40] Application cost: "depends, 50, 75,000 maybe" (hard costs such as schematic design, environmentals, market study; staff time not tracked).
- [58:30] Success rate: "probably successful … 80% of the time maybe". ACTION usually submits at most two per round, one City and one County.
- [60:25–60:52] "the allocation to us would be 1.2 million, you take that over 10 years, simple math 12 million, but that's not necessarily the amount of equity we get … as low as 91 cents per dollar … as high as a dollar and six … we had a $1.2 million allocation that generated just under $1,700,000 in equity". ⚠ ASR is ambiguous here. "$1.7M" is inconsistent with a $12M credit stream priced at $0.91–$1.06, so the spoken figure was more likely about $11.7M. **Unverified.**
- [61:15] "10 years that they get the tax credit but there's a 15-year compliance period".
- [16:00–16:40] Parking: "every single project we build parking that doesn't get used … maybe half of our tenants have cars".
- [20:50] Required parking "doesn't decrease it a lot … the funding source … works best for projects of a certain size, so that's really the driver".

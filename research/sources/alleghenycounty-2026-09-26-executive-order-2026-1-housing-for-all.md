Section 2. Housing Needs Assessment
Allegheny County shall, through Allegheny County Economic Development shall and with
guidance from the Director of Housing Strategy, undertake the County’s first Housing Needs
Assessment which will:
   •   Investigate the financial, regulatory, and land use barriers that slow or deter
       housing production across all income levels;
   •   Determine a realistic representation of Allegheny County’s housing shortage
       across all income levels, geographic areas, and household sizes;
   •   Explore all currently available financing and investment tools available to
       Allegheny County, the Redevelopment Authority of Allegheny County, the
       Allegheny County Housing Authority, and the Residential Finance Authority to
       increase the production of affordable homes, for both rental and homeownership,
       throughout Allegheny County;
   •   Make recommendations for policy reform and public investment to rapidly
       address housing shortages throughout Allegheny County.
Section 3. Land Recycling and Blight Remediation
Allegheny County shall undertake a thorough review of existing land recycling and land
banking activities throughout Allegheny County to create a recommended strategy to increase
the scale of land recycling and blight remediation throughout Allegheny County. This work
shall be led by Allegheny County Economic Development, with support from the Director of
Housing Strategy.
This strategy should build off local successes and be grounded in proven national examples
that will restore our municipal tax bases, create more homes, and strengthen families and
communities.
Section 4. Expanding Housing Supply
       1. Investment in the Housing Supply
To align with the findings of the Housing Needs Assessment, Allegheny County Economic
Development shall study national best practices and recommend feasible and catalytic
public/private investment strategies that can sustain the immense progress of 500 in 500 and
increase housing production throughout Allegheny County across the economic spectrum.
Due to expected federal cuts from the Trump Administration during FY 26 and FY 27 these
funding strategies should set an ambitious target of raising between $50 million - $100 million
over the coming years. Recommendations on fund uses, governance, and capital raising shall be
prepared by June 1, 2026.
Allegheny County, through our relevant authorities, the County Manager’s Office, and
Allegheny County Economic Development shall court a broad and diverse investor pool to
build this fund, including corporate and nonprofit partners, our lending community, our
philanthropic partners, and union investment vehicles such as the ERECT Funds and the AFL-
CIO Housing Investment Trust.
All funding strategies shall be developed with a specific focus on deeply affordable (under 60%
AMI) and workforce (under 120% AMI) housing options with clear operational plans that can
position the recommended fund to be self-sustaining in the long-term.
To the maximum extent feasible, Allegheny County Economic Development, shall align
housing investment strategies and priorities with planned or underway job creation/retention
and business development investments.
       2. Financialization of Housing
Allegheny County shall work with all relevant internal and external partners to assess and make
recommendations about promising policy interventions and best practices to push back on the
increasing scale of private equity activity in the single-family housing market in order to

preserve housing options for Allegheny County residents. This work should include
recommendations to support any required state or federal legislation to level the playing field
and make homeownership more accessible for more Allegheny County residents.
       3. Regulatory Barriers
Allegheny County, through Allegheny County Economic Development, shall use the ongoing
Comprehensive Plan process to create a library of template ordinances and zoning code
amendments to support any municipality who wishes to pursue zoning reform and
modernization to support and promote housing development.
Through the Housing Needs Assessment, Allegheny County shall develop a suite of
recommendations to support the modernization of municipal permit review processes for
housing development.
Section 5. Publicly Owned Assets for Housing Production
The County Manager and Director of Housing Strategy shall work with relevant County
departments to review all County-owned property and identify sites that may be suitable for housing
development and where additional housing development would support existing residents and new
movers, as well as existing employment opportunities and future job creation. This review shall
produce a rank-ordering of County-owned real estate assets based on the feasibility of housing
development by December 1st, 2026.
All sites shall be reviewed in the context of the investment studies and recommendations to be
promulgated by Allegheny County Economic Development and shall consider the economic and
operational feasibility of private development, public/private joint-ventures, and fully public
development.
Section 6. Strengthening Supports for Existing Residents
       1. Homeowner Protections
  The County Manager, County Law Department, and the Office of Management and Budget
  are hereby directed to investigate and make recommendations to protect long-time and
  low/moderate income homeowners from potential economic shock of any reassessment.
  Policies such as Long Time Owner Occupant Protection and an increase in the Homestead
  Exemption shall be studied, modeled, and recommended to County Council for adoption.
       2. Owner Occupied Home Rehabilitation
  Allegheny County, through Allegheny County Economic Development, shall invest no less
  than $1.5 million into home repair services for low- and moderate-income homeowners
  throughout Allegheny County in 2026. Furthermore, all relevant County departments shall
  work with State and Federal partners to create and unlock new resources for this system.
       3. Lead Safe Homes
  Allegheny County shall seek all available state and federal funding to sustain, and expand
  where possible, existing programs led by Allegheny County Economic Development and
  their relevant authorities that address lead remediation, home repair, and home modifications
  that allow county residents to securely and safely remain in their homes and their
  communities.
       4. Tenant Protections
  Allegheny County, through the Allegheny County Health Department, and their Housing
  Advisory Committee, the County Manager, County Law Department, and Director of
  Housing Strategy shall work to advance enforceable and impactful legislative updates that
  will protect tenants’ right to organize, their right to report unsafe and unhealthy housing, and
  other key issues that support tenants’ housing stability and health. This work should build on

  work done previously by Pittsburgh’s Housing Justice Table, Eviction Prevention Working
  Group, and other key local and statewide practitioners and experts.
  Furthermore, Allegheny County Economic Development and the Allegheny County
  Housing Authority are instructed to work with the previously mentioned internal partners to
  understand how tenant protections can be required in locally funded housing developments.
Section 7. Sustaining Success
       1. 500 and 500
 All County Departments and Authorities shall work to ensure that the cross-departmental and
 multi-governmental approaches that were developed as part of 500 in 500 remain in place and
 are built upon to continue to serve our most at-risk neighbors. Allegheny County Economic
 Development, Allegheny County Housing Authority, and other partners shall continue to
 explore how best to leverage public investment in affordable housing to create set asides in
 new developments to support our unhoused neighbors.
       2. 1st Home Allegheny
 Allegheny County Economic Development and/or their relevant authorities shall investigate
 and make recommendations for long-term sustainable funding for 1st Home Allegheny with a
 goal of supporting up to 50 first time homebuyers each year.
       3. Preserving Existing Affordable Housing
 Consistent with the community priorities identified in All in Allegheny, Allegheny County
 Housing Authority is encouraged to continue and expand efforts to acquire properties with
 expiring affordability requirements to preserve housing options, extend the benefits of public
 investment, and prevent displacement. This work should be undertaken in collaboration with
 Allegheny County Economic Development and Allegheny County Department of Human
 Services to ensure to the maximum extent feasible that acquisitions align with shortcomings
 identified in the Housing Needs Assessment and will support housing insecure residents.
       4. Eviction Prevention
 Allegheny County Economic Development, Allegheny County Department of Human
 Services, and the County Manager’s Office shall work together to create reliable annual
 funding for Allegheny County’s Eviction Prevention system. All relevant County departments
 shall work with State and Federal partners to create and unlock new resources for this system.
       5. Mental Health Housing
 Allegheny County Department of Human Services shall reinvest savings from mental health
 housing and other reforms into a solicitation to procure housing solutions for people living
 with and recovering from substance use disorder, serious mental illness, and homelessness
 with a goal of creating up to 200 additional housing units for these populations.
Section 8. Preserving and Protecting Housing Quality and Health
       1. Emergency Housing Strike Team
Recognizing that often tenants’ housing security is at the mercy of their landlord, Allegheny
County shall work proactively to determine properties at risk of displacement. The County
Manager shall convene an Emergency Housing Strike Team that includes the Department of
Human Services, the Department of Emergency Management, the Allegheny County Health
Department, the Allegheny County Law Department, and any other relevant departments and
authorities to create, and update on at least an annual basis, a list of multi-family rental
properties at risk of displacement based on a combination of the below:
           a. Public Health Code Violations
           b. Municipal Code Violations
                                               5
           c. Delinquent Taxes
           d. Expiration of existing housing subsidy
           e. Market Characteristics
           f. Other key metrics as identified in the Housing Needs Assessment
Upon finalization of this list, the County Manager shall notify all property owners of their
placement on this list and begin proactive coordination with the Department of Human
Services’ Housing Team and coordinate any and all appropriate additional inspections through
the Allegheny County Health Department, and position Allegheny County to be made
financially whole for any and all work to house tenants displaced at any of the identified
properties.
   2. Protecting Tenant, Community, and Environmental Health
Allegheny County Health Department, with the guidance of the Board of Health’s Housing
Advisory Committee, shall continue to explore national best practices and make
recommendations to secure tenant health and stability through modernized policy and
enforcement of Article 6 of the Allegheny County Health Department Rules and Regulations.
Furthermore, the Health Department shall take all necessary steps to ensure that the recently
passed “Clean and Lien” update to Article 6 can be adequately and efficiently implemented.
Section 9. Inter-Governmental Cooperation
The challenges of housing affordability, security, and stability are multi-dimensional and require a
multi-faceted, coordinated response. The County Manager shall take all reasonable steps to ensure
that the inter-departmental and intergovernmental cooperation that has led to the success of
multiple new housing initiatives is sustained and expanded into the future.
Section 10. Implementation
The County Manager and County agencies under the County Executive’s jurisdiction are
directed to take all necessary steps to implement this Executive Order. County-affiliated or
related entities are encouraged to make a good faith effort to advance the goals of this
Executive Order. All actions taken shall be in accordance with applicable local, state, and
federal laws.
Section 11. Effective Date
This Executive Order shall take effect immediately and shall remain in effect until amended or
rescinded by the Chief Executive.

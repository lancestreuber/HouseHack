# Critique: an adversarial audit of the research base

*Written 2026-09-26, during the build window. This is the critique pass the paper promises. It was written by an agent asked to find what is wrong, overclaimed or missing, and it is deliberately one-sided. Where it confirms something, it says so. Live checks are listed in §6, and every "I checked" below refers to one of them.*

---

## 1. The assessment

**What the base actually establishes.** The base has a solid, reproducible grip on *public data plumbing*. Endpoints exist, they return what the nodes say they return, and the counts match. I re-queried several of them and they held exactly: 584,999 assessment rows, 5,786 city-owned vacant parcels with 3,260 "Available for Sale", 330,166 OneStopPGH records, 490/370 residential/commercial new-construction permits, 583 recoded records, 6,657 sale-code-16 sales, and 92 "Insufficient Data" DRR block groups. The §903.03 dimensional table also matches the live eCode360 text. That is real work, and it is the part of the base a builder can lean on.

**What it appears to establish but does not.** The executive summary reads as findings about *housing in Pittsburgh*: where friction sits, who is at displacement risk, what practitioners need, and how much buildable public land exists. Almost none of those is established. Where the base did measure something, the measurement is weaker than it looks. The headline permit-timeline numbers mix applicant time with City time, and they count parallel reviewer flags as "revision cycles" (§2, rows 2–3). The equity headline is contradicted by the one displacement dataset the base cites (row 24). The "available lots" headline says nothing about whether the lots are buildable, and about half of them are under 2,400 sf (row 18). The practitioner section ranks barriers as though a survey had been done, but it is one agent's ordering of about a dozen news articles (row 12).

**Source-type monoculture.** The evidence comes from three kinds of source: ArcGIS/WPRDC endpoints, City web pages, and local journalism (WESA, PublicSource, Post-Gazette, Axios). Two more kinds carry load-bearing claims without being flagged as distinct and weaker:
- **citizenportal.ai.** An automated summary site. It is the only source for the Land Bank's "9 months to title", the rehab pilot terms, and the Sept 2025 hearing testimony, and it is tagged `[read]` each time.
- **Commercial mirrors (zoneomics).** They carry Chapters 906, 914, 915 and 922.

The base has **zero** primary legislative records (Legistar), **zero** meeting minutes, **zero** interviews, **zero** full academic papers, and **one** parsed ZBA decision. The legislative gap was *not* an access problem. Pittsburgh's Legistar web API answered on the first unauthenticated request (§6).

**Method artifacts.** Everything was produced in one day by agents:
- Sweeps were run by research agents.
- Nodes were written by another agent summarizing the sweeps.
- The paper summarizes the nodes.
- Tags were assigned by the same agents that did the reading.

So a node's `[read]` frequently means *"a sweep agent reported that it read this"*. Several nodes say so outright ("`[read]` per the data sweep", "read from the PDF by the data-sweep agent"), and nobody re-opened the artefact. Each layer of summarizing drops caveats:
- "the sweep's judgment, not a survey" (practitioners node) becomes an ordered list in the paper.
- "the agent's judgment … not tested against our own data" (displacement node) becomes a flat claim in the paper.
- "no public data found (unverified beyond search)" (infrastructure node) becomes "No … capacity data exists" in the paper.

The bibliography assigns each source its *strongest* tag across nodes. That systematically launders disagreements upward: Lenze et al. shows as `[read]` although the source-access log says abstract-only and the ZBA node says it could not be found. The corrections log records nine corrections, and every one of them weakened a claim. Nothing suggests the pass that produced them was exhaustive. This audit found about thirty more.

## 3. Voice drift

These are places where an inference, a guess or one agent's judgment is written as though a source said it.

- **Paper §6:** "Nonprofits often work parcels they already control." The practitioners node labels this "Sweep inference from the sources above, not a quote." The paper drops the label.
- **Paper §6 / Part III:** the ranked barrier list. The node's caveat ("not a survey") is gone in the paper.
- **Paper §12:** "will tend to point at … where displacement risk is highest." The node calls this "the agent's judgment … not tested against our own data". In the paper it reads as a finding, and the data contradict it (row 24). The displacement node's phrase "Transitional and Stressed **Black** neighborhoods" turns a population share (45% / 21% of Black residents) into a racial label for places.
- **Paper §7:** "The City is growth-first", on the strength of one deputy chief of staff quote in one article.
- **Paper §9:** "Sewer capacity is decided per project through the DEP planning-module process." The source is a `[skimmed]` PWSA page, and "no planning exemptions since 2011" is a sweep's paraphrase. It is written as settled process.
- **Paper §10:** "That is enough to report how well a rule-based score ranks parcels." This is a sweep's estimate (an uncomputed ±0.03 CI and events-per-variable from memory), stated as a finding.
- **Paper §11:** "operational energy falls steeply with density." RECS reports averages by building type. "With density" is our causal gloss.
- **Paper §14:** "**The build is feasible** on the team's stack." The caveat ("recommendations from one feasibility sweep, not tested builds") comes after a headline that asserts feasibility.
- **Carbon node:** "Per unit or per bedroom the order can flip." This is the sweep's reading of the Dublin paper, and no per-unit figures are given.
- **ZBA node:** "Regex-friendly", generalized from one parsed decision.
- **Land Bank node:** "the most plausible pilot partner in the sweeps". This is a sweep ranking, and the node admits "no one there has confirmed demand."
- **Approval-pathway node:** "The ≥4-unit threshold appears three times … so 3→4 units is a step change in process." Two of those three thresholds are mirror-read or `[skimmed]`.
- **Practitioners node:** the permit median "agrees" with an anecdote (row 33).

## 4. Missing source types

Each item below is a kind of evidence the base did not reach, with exactly where to get it.

1. **City Council legislative records.** The base says it has none. Pittsburgh Legistar has a keyless web API (`webapi.legistar.com/v1/pittsburgh/matters?$filter=MatterFile eq '2025-1545'`, then `/matters/{id}/histories` and `/attachments`), and the portal is pittsburgh.legistar.com. It holds status, action history, the **"VERSION 2 PLEASE USE FOR FULL TEXT"** bill PDF, and the **June 2, 2026 PC recommendation redline**. Use it for 2025-1545, 2026-0834, 2025-1579 and 2024-0701, and read the redlines *visually* (strikethrough).
2. **ZBA decisions at scale.** Crawl the per-meeting pages (`…/City-Planning-Meetings/ZBA-Agendas/ZBA-<date>`) with a real browser or browser-like client across several years. Parse outcome, sections varied and BDA number, then join to OSPI_H. This is the only route to approval rates and to which variances block small infill.
3. **The Title Nine chapters that decide buildability.** Read these on eCode360 in a browser, since my check shows Chrome gets through:
   - **Ch. 921 Nonconformities** (lots of record: decides whether the ~36% of sub-minimum available lots are buildable)
   - §912 accessory uses
   - §907.04 IZ-O
   - §915.07 Performance Points (the bonus is being built on it)
   - §906.02 floodway text and the §906.08 setback standard
4. **Practitioners.** Slack office hours, Sat/Sun 10–6 ET. Ask the permitting and planning mentors, and if reachable a CDC and a small builder, the Tier-1 questions in the open-question register. Add the event partner Pro-Housing Pittsburgh. This is the only way to test §6 and §12 of the paper.
5. **Land Bank primary records.** Board agendas and minutes, instead of citizenportal.ai. The Land Bank site failed for curl, so try a browser. This is needed to confirm the "9 months" figure and the rehab pilot terms.
6. **Reinvestment Fund MVA/DRR methodology.** Sources: the "MVA Presentation" PDF and the public info-session recording on the same WPRDC dataset page (both unread); Reinvestment Fund's PolicyMap/MVA methodology documentation; or ask Reinvestment Fund directly. The DRR's behavior in §2 row 24 makes this urgent.
7. **Allegheny County Housing Needs Assessment.** The catalog URL is dead. Ask the organizers which document they meant. Read the County Executive's Feb 5, 2026 executive order text on the County site, not WESA's summary.
8. **Infrastructure procedures.** PWSA developer manual and Water & Sewer Use Application guidance (pgh2o.com developers section); DEP Act 537 planning-module guidance; ALCOSAN's consent-decree documents. These would turn "unknown" into a documented *process* per project size, even though capacity data is not public.
9. **Devpost and hackathon project archives.** Use a real browser (scripted search is blocked). Seattle, NJ and AEC Tech entry pages give what was actually built rather than press summaries.

## 5. What would most change the conclusions

1. **Re-computing permit timelines correctly.** Count rounds as distinct review starts, split applicant time from City time, and handle censoring (Kaplan–Meier over the 210). If most of the 153+ days is applicant time or completeness churn, the "friction" story changes from *City/discretionary delay* to *application quality*. That points the product at pre-submission checking, which is the one AI use the City has named.
2. **A practitioner or mentor telling us who the user is and where projects stall.** Every framing choice rests on news-article barrier lists. One planning mentor could overturn §6 and §7.
3. **Buildability of the public-lot inventory.** Ch. 921 lot-of-record rules plus lot size, slope and H-district status applied to the 3,260 available lots. If most sub-minimum lots are unbuildable, an "available lots" demo points at unusable land. If most are buildable as lots of record, that becomes a strong, checkable feature.
4. **Understanding the DRR, or replacing it.** The base's only displacement dataset flags Robust markets, not the Transitional and Stressed ones the paper warns about. Either the DRR measures price appreciation relative to the county (so it is the wrong warning signal), or the paper's hazard claim is wrong. The Track 3 equity design depends on which.
5. **Primary text of Bills 2025-1545 (June 2026 redline) and 2026-0834.** The latter rewrites Ch. 906, 921 and 922, the very rules the gates and pathway engine would encode. A tool pinned to "current code" may be scoring against rules that are weeks from replacement, and the ADU/parking/IZ terms in the base come from 2024 pages.

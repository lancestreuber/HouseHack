# Environmental overlays (Chapter 906) and Ch. 915 standards

**Type:** policy
**One line:** The steep-slope (SS-O), landslide-prone (LS-O), undermined (UM-O) and floodplain (FP-O) overlay districts in Chapter 906, plus the general slope and tree rules in §915.02.
**Why we care:** Pittsburgh's hills make these the most common site-level blockers after zoning. Some add months of Planning Commission review; floodway and shallow undermining are close to no-build.
**Last checked:** 2026-09-26

⚠ **Correction:** earlier notes put these rules in Chapter 915. They are the **Chapter 906 overlay districts**. Ch. 915 holds the general environmental performance standards. See the corrections log in [../README.md](../README.md).

## The overlays

| Overlay | Trigger | Who decides | What the code says (eCode360, read 2026-09-26) | Added time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SS-O Steep Slope §906.08** | Land with "a natural slope of twenty-five (25) percent or greater" | **Planning Commission review for all development** `[read]` | ≥21-day notice to owners within 150 ft (≥25 nearest); hearing within 60 days of a complete application; decision within 45 days of the hearing; **miss = deemed denial**. 14 review standards (C.5.1–14) on top of §915.02 and Ch. 916, including: development "set back fifty (50) feet **in both directions** from the edge of the SS-O boundary when it occurs at either the Ridgeline or Base" (so into and out of the overlay). "The Planning Commission may waive any of the above standards if finds that other mitigating measures are taken", so the 50 ft setback is **waivable**. No outright ban. Approval lapses after 1 year. `[read]` *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)* | +2–4 months (sweep estimate); statutory ceiling ~3.5 months before deemed denial |
| **LS-O Landslide-prone §906.04** | Parcel in mapped LS-O and the work involves excavation, fill or vegetation removal | Zoning Administrator (on a registered professional's field investigation); PLI Building Chief approves construction and land-operations plans | Evidence from a field investigation required. **Also:** every new or changed use and every structure erected or enlarged "shall comply with the Hillside Development Standards of the Subdivision Regulations" (not read), and a development plan is required for the Certificate of Occupancy (B.1). B.3 cites "Section 906.03.B.2", apparently a stale cross-reference. `[read]` *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)* | + geotech study, weeks (estimate) |
| **UM-O Undermined §906.05** | Parcel in UM-O, new construction or enlargement | Zoning Administrator plus Building Chief | **Single-unit dwelling allowed if >100 ft of overburden and no subsidence history.** Anything larger or heavier, or overburden <100 ft, is **prohibited until a site investigation shows it is safe** ("Other Development Prohibited"), which covers "any structure that is larger or heavier than a typical single-unit dwelling" and hazardous uses. So any 2+ unit building larger than a typical house needs a site investigation (sweep's reading). DEP mine data required for all new construction or enlargement (B.1). `[read]` | + study |
| **FP-O Floodplain §906.02** | FIS/FIRMs "dated September 26, 2014 … or the most recent revision thereof" (A/AE zones), **plus** "Community Identified Flood Hazard Areas" on a Local Flood Hazard Map | Zoning Administrator plus a Bureau of Building Inspection permit (the code does not say "Floodplain Permit"; that is the OneStopPGH record type) | Lowest floor at or above the **regulatory flood elevation = base flood elevation + 1.5 ft** freeboard (J.2(aa)). **Floodway: no encroachment unless a hydrologic and hydraulic analysis shows no increase in flood levels, AND no new construction without a DEP permit** (E.2(a)). §906.02.I.1(a) and §922.09.F.4 bar variances that would raise floodway flood levels. The sweep reads the floodway as effectively no-build (inference). `[read]` *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)* | + weeks |

## §915.02 environmental standards (all sites)

*(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)* All `[read]` on eCode360 ([saved text](../../sources/ecode360-2026-09-26-pittsburgh-915-environmental-performance.md)). §915.01.B applies Ch. 915 to all structures and land uses.

- **Cut or fill slopes** "shall not exceed twenty-five (25) percent unless" there is **both** a geotechnical investigation report **and** walls, terraces or similar at intervals of not less than 10 ft (A.1.c). Applies to all slopes, not only SS-O.
- Finished grades ≤15% are "strongly encouraged" (A.1.c). Top and bottom of cut/fill slopes ≥5 ft from any property line, street, building or parking area (A.1.d).
- **Retaining walls** or gabions "shall not exceed ten (10) feet in height" (A.1.e).
- Slopes >15% exposed during construction must be revegetated: 1 canopy tree, 2 evergreens, 2 understory trees and 5 shrubs per 150 sf (B).
- **Tree survey** for sites >¼ acre (10,890 sf) (C).
- ⚠ **Tree replacement, corrected:** protected trees are those "with a diameter of twelve (12) inches or more, measured at a point four (4) feet above grade" (not the standard 4.5 ft DBH). Removed trees must be "replaced, at a minimum, equal to the **combined total diameter** of removed trees" (D). So replacement is by aggregate diameter, **not one-for-one**.
- Decided by the Zoning Administrator (C, D).

⚠ **"40% no-disturbance / 30% max disturbance of 25–40% slopes" — NOT FOUND** *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)*. It is not in §906.08, §915.01 or §915.02; the only numeric slope thresholds there are 15% and 25%. The full-code search did not load, so it may live elsewhere, most plausibly the Subdivision Regulations' Hillside Development Standards that §906.04 cites (open question). Keep it out of the rules engine.

## Why it matters in practice

- Landslides destroyed a home on Greenleaf St in 2018 and closed William St on Mount Washington; the William St fix is a roughly $10M FEMA-backed project `[skimmed]` ([PublicSource on landslides](https://www.publicsource.org/landslides-pittsburgh-mount-washington-federal-funds-mayor-gainey-traffic/), [WESA 2024-10-25](https://www.wesa.fm/development-transportation/2024-10-25/pittsburgh-landslides-flooding-insurance)).
- A builder blog puts hillside site work at $30K–80K extra `[skimmed]` ([r1 prior-art sweep](../../sweeps/r1-prior-art-proforma-and-practitioner-barriers.md)).
- The EZ Permit one-day track excludes floodplain parcels `[read]` ([approval pathway](approval-pathway.md)).

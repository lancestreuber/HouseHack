# Approval pathway

**Type:** policy
**One line:** Every approval step a Pittsburgh housing project can hit, what triggers it, who decides, how long it takes and what it costs, with a verification tag per row.
**Why we care:** "Ease" is mostly about which discretionary steps a parcel-plus-project triggers. This table is the rules engine's spec.
**Last checked:** 2026-09-26

This is working research, not legal advice. Confirm with City Planning or mentors.

⚠ **Conflict: state law may make missed deadlines deemed approvals** *(added 2026-09-26, legal-feasibility review)*. PA MPC §908(9) and §913.2(b)(2) (DCED 2003 edition, `[read]`) say that when the board or governing body misses its hearing or decision deadline, the decision is "deemed to have been rendered in favor of the applicant". Legistar shows 39 Pittsburgh conditional uses "Passed pursuant to Case Law" ([Council actions](../../datasets/legal-feasibility/council-land-use-actions.md)). Which rule controls in Pittsburgh is **unresolved**; the City-code text below is kept as read. → [MPC excerpt](../../sources/pa-dced-2003-01-mpc-908-913-2-deemed-approval.md)

⚠ **Missed deadlines are deemed denials, not approvals** *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)*. For SS-O (§906.08.C.4), Conditional Use (§922.06.C, D.1), Special Exception (§922.07.C), Variance (§922.09.D) and Administrator Exception (§922.08.C), if the decider misses the hearing or decision deadline, "the decision shall be deemed to have been rendered in denial" unless the applicant agreed to an extension. A deemed-denied AE "may be sent to the Zoning Board of Adjustment for immediate review". `[read]` The one exception: under Site Plan Review (§922.04.D), Planning Commission silence for 60 days is "deemed to be a recommendation for approval" (advisory only). ⚠ Bill 2026-0834 (hearing 10/13/26) would amend Ch. 906, 915, 921 and **922**, so most rows here may change *(corrected 2026-09-26 per docs/04-critique.md row 8)*.

## ZBA specifics

- From the ZBA process handout (Dec 2024) `[read]`: $400 fee, ≥21-day posted notice, hearings on the first 3 Thursdays of each month, decision within 45 days after the record closes, **appeal to Common Pleas within 30 days**, **approval expires in 1 year**.
- **2026 sample of 90 decisions** *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)* `[read]` ([ZBA decisions](../data/zba-decisions.md), [sweep](../../sweeps/r5-zba-decisions-sample.md)): of 84 applicant relief requests, **81% approved** (35 with conditions), **15.5% denied**, **3.6% split**. Riskiest: signs (40% denied), front-yard parking pads (33%). Safest: §921.02 nonconforming use changes (14/14 approved), §916 compatibility waivers (9/9), §914 parking (8/8). Two-unit in single-unit or H districts: 3 approved, 2 denied. 21% of cases add housing units. Final hearing → decision median 34 days (IQR 26–41). ⚠ Conditional on a decision being posted: withdrawals are invisible and ~⅓ of 2026 case numbers are missing.
- One parsed decision (1137 Pennsylvania Ave) had 44 days between hearing and decision `[read]`. Decision PDFs carry labeled fields (hearing date, decision date, zone case, address, lot/block, district, request, **BDA application number**, variance/SE table by section, decision paragraph) and join to OneStopPGH via the BDA number. See [ZBA decisions data](../data/zba-decisions.md).
- **Volume:** 210 ZBA applications in 2020, per the abstract of Lenze, Hinojos and Grady 2024 `[skimmed]` ⚠ *(corrected 2026-09-26 per docs/04-critique.md row 29)*; the r2 sweep's own estimate is ~150–200 a year from ~5 cases per meeting `[unverified estimate]`.
- **Approval rates: none published** anywhere the sweeps searched; the numbers above are our own sample. PublicSource Board Explorer shows members and cadence only `[read]`.
- **Contradiction:** the r1 prior-art sweep reported Lenze et al. 2024 (210 applications from 2020, no significant overall relationship with a social vulnerability index, but significant links with the share of pre-1940 housing and of multifamily units). The r2 sweep reported it **could not find** that paper. ⚠ The source-access log says only the **abstract** was reached, so the tag is `[skimmed]` and these findings are **per the abstract** *(corrected 2026-09-26 per docs/04-critique.md row 29)*. The r2 miss is a search failure, not a contrary finding.

## How a rules engine should use this (inference)

- Map parcel attributes (district, overlays, slope, flood, historic, lot size) plus project attributes (units, GFA, parking, disturbance) to the set of triggered rows.
- If the target unit count exceeds the district's by-right ceiling, or any dimensional minimum isn't met, output "Variance / ZBA".
- ⚠ If the [parking](parking.md) minimum isn't met, output **"Alternative Access and Parking Plan: Zoning Administrator if ≤10 spaces required; ZBA Special Exception if >10"** (§914.07.B, D), not "Variance". Transit-stop reduction is capped at 20% *(corrected 2026-09-26, round 5)* `[read]`.
- Per the City code, a missed decision deadline is a **deemed denial**. ⚠ PA MPC §908(9)/§913.2(b)(2) say the opposite (deemed approval), and this is unresolved (see top). The engine should model neither; show the deadline and flag the conflict *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)*.
- Use the ZBA sample's outcome rates by relief type as a rough prior for "how risky is this relief", with the selection-bias caveat *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5; inference)*.
- The ≥4-unit threshold appears in Site Plan Review (§922.04, now `[read]`) and the RCO DAM (`[skimmed]`), so 3→4 units is likely a step change in process *(qualified 2026-09-26 per docs/04-critique.md §3)*. ⚠ PWSA's trigger is different: a development permit is needed for anything beyond a single-family tap (e.g. >2 tap terminations, subdivided lots), not at 4 units *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)*.
- Pin a code version: several rows may change with [pending reforms](reforms-in-flux-2025-2026.md), including who runs community meetings.

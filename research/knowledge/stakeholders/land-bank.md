# Pittsburgh Land Bank

**Type:** stakeholder
**One line:** The City-adjacent Pittsburgh Land Bank (pghlandbank.org): its inventory, disposition rules, sheriff-sale authority, rehab pilot, and funding cliff *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)*.
**Why we care:** Acquisition and title are the bottleneck practitioners name most. The Land Bank is the body that shortens them, and it holds exactly the vacant and condemned inventory an "ease" score would rank. It is the most plausible pilot partner in the sweeps, though no one there has confirmed demand.
**Last checked:** 2026-09-26

**Official site:** [pghlandbank.org](https://pghlandbank.org/) *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)*. `pittsburghlandbank.org` did not resolve in DNS `[inaccessible]`. ⚠ **Name trap:** search results for "PLB Board … Approved Meeting Minutes for April 14, 2026" (with an "EOI process" and a "disposition policy not updated since January 2020") are the **Philadelphia** Land Bank; the PDF header reads "PHILADELPHIA LAND BANK" `[read]`. ## Disposition policy *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)* `[read]`

From the [2022 Disposition Process](https://pghlandbank.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/PLB-Disposition-Process_2022.pdf), [Policies & Procedures amended 4/6/2022](https://pghlandbank.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/2022-PLB-Policies-Procedures-Amendment-4.6.22_FINAL.pdf) and the [2024 Annual Report](https://pghlandbank.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Pittsburgh-Land-Bank-2024-Annual-Report-1.pdf) (verbatim excerpts in [pghlandbank-2026-09-26-disposition-policy-and-2026-board-records.md](../../sources/pghlandbank-2026-09-26-disposition-policy-and-2026-board-records.md)):

- **Steps:** Property Transfer Application → sale and end user approved by a **two-thirds Board vote** → online notice and on-site signage **≥30 days** before the Purchase and Sale Agreement → **20-day objection window**; a petition from **15 or more** interested parties triggers a neighborhood public hearing → 30 days to sign the PSA → closing within **6 months**.
- **Direct (noncompetitive) sales**, adopted 2022 and implemented 2023, for: **side yards** (adjacent owner-occupants, max 2); **housing with ≥25% of units at ≤80% AMI**; Property Reserve; community facilities; assemblages (applicant owns ≥50% of the site); nonprofit green space. Still need a two-thirds vote of the entire Board.
- **Price:** "good and valuable consideration", determined by the market, unless a policy exception applies (P&P 5.02(F)).
- **Affordability goal:** the City–URA–PLB Tri-Party Agreement (rev. 10/18/23) §7(g) sets a goal that **80% of PLB dispositions** be for affordable housing (rentals ≤50% AMI, for-sale ≤80% AMI). §17 requires reporting of properties held and transferred and units created; those reports were not found.
- Implication (inference): a Land Bank lot is not "available" in the way a listing is. Any disposition carries at least ~30 days of notice plus a 20-day objection window and a board vote, and most are expected to serve affordable housing.

## Title clearing and acquisition

- In April 2026 the Land Bank started using **sheriff-sale authority**. ⚠ It **expects** about **9 months to a free-and-clear title, versus about 2 years before**. This is a stated expectation, not a measured result; there is no outcome data *(corrected 2026-09-26 per docs/04-critique.md row 13)*. `[read]` ([citizenportal.ai summary](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/7977800/pennsylvania/allegheny-county/pittsburgh/pittsburgh-land-bank-approves-two-sales-moves-to-speed-acquisitions-and-expand-rehab-pilot), a third-party summary). WESA (2025-11-20) described the same 2-years-to-9-months change as coming from "a new agreement" `[read]`. citizenportal.ai is an automated meeting-summary site, a weaker source type than minutes. *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)* The task force report confirms the agreement: City, PLB, Allegheny County and Pittsburgh Public Schools signed a Sheriff's Sale access agreement in **November 2025**, "now fully executed" `[read]`, and the Sept 2026 agenda includes a Sheriff's Sale acquisition. The 9-month expectation is still unmeasured.
- It mostly acquires parcels where a buyer is already identified `[skimmed]` ([PCRG](https://www.pcrg.org/pittsburgh-land-bank)).
- The City "is not foreclosing on tax-delinquent vacant homes", so "these properties are simply inaccessible" `[read]` ([PublicSource 2025-12-22](https://www.publicsource.org/pittsburgh-housing-shortage-population-decline-vacant-homes/)).

## Rehab pilot

- A **residential rehab pilot** for owner-occupants and small developers is expanding; applicants must show **proof of funding and a rehab plan** `[read]` (citizenportal.ai summary).
- The Land Bank's manager: "Every month… the rehab becomes more and more expensive." `[read]` ([WESA 2026-01-15](https://www.wesanews.org/development-transportation/2026-01-15/pittsburgh-land-bank-funding-blight)).

## Funding

- The Jan 2026 task force asked for **$32M a year for 5 years**; federal funding runs out after 2027 `[read]` ([WESA 2026-01-15](https://www.wesanews.org/development-transportation/2026-01-15/pittsburgh-land-bank-funding-blight)).
- The Land Bank has received **no City money since its initial $3.5M in 2021** `[read]` ([PublicSource 2025-12-22](https://www.publicsource.org/pittsburgh-housing-shortage-population-decline-vacant-homes/)).
- WESA (2025-11-20): "no dedicated city funding after 2027" `[read]`.

**Tension, not resolved:** PublicSource says no City money since 2021; WESA frames the cliff as "no dedicated city funding after 2027" and the Jan 2026 piece says *federal* funding runs out after 2027. *(updated 2026-09-26, round 5)* The task force report itself says ARPA, "the PLB's primary revenue source up to this point, must be **spent by the end of Fiscal Year 2026**" `[read]`. Spend-by (FY2026) and runs-out-after (2027) may both be true if obligations stretch; unreconciled. Most likely reading (inference): the Land Bank has run on federal ARPA money since the initial City grant. We have not seen the budget.

## What this means for us (inference)

- A disposition prioritizer over the Land Bank / City inventory (which lots to clear title on and market first, and for what use) is one of the stronger framings the alternative-framings sweep found. Its main risk: the Land Bank's own priorities (funding runway, community goals) may differ from "ease", and the inventory data is known to be messy.
- Most of this inventory is rehab of condemned structures or small, irregular vacant lots, so condition and cost-of-waiting belong in a score, probably as a separate track from vacant-lot infill.

## Open questions

- Would a scored inventory be useful to Land Bank staff? Who owns that inventory's data today? (No machine-readable PLB list is published; parcel IDs are only in board resolutions.)
- Are the Tri-Party §17 reports (properties held, transferred, units created) public?
- Is there data on acquisition status (treasurer or sheriff sale pipeline, liens, conservatorship) we could use or mock?
- Current Land Bank budget and funding sources.
- What condition data exists for condemned structures?

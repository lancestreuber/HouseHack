# State: DCED and PHFA

**Type:** stakeholder
**One line:** What Pennsylvania's Department of Community and Economic Development (DCED) and the Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency (PHFA) have said about housing supply, regulation and funding priorities.
**Why we care:** The state sets the funding rules (PHARE, LIHTC via the QAP) that make affordable projects pencil, and its Housing Action Plan calls for modernized municipal regulation. A PHFA-funded fellow is studying exactly the vacant-lot question Track 1 asks.
**Last checked:** 2026-09-26

## DCED: PA Housing Action Plan 2026–2035

Read from the **summary page only**, not the full PDF `[read]` ([DCED](https://dced.pa.gov/housing-and-development/pennsylvania-housing-action-plan/)):

- The state needs **450,000 units by 2035**; the current trend falls about **185,000 short**.
- Recommends modernizing regulations and the **Municipalities Planning Code**, and cross-agency data sharing.
- Emphasizes repair and reinvestment in existing homes (over 50% of PA housing stock is more than 50 years old), land banks, and workforce training.
- A "housing one-stop shop."
- Proposes $1M for the State Planning Board to help municipalities with zoning and permitting `[skimmed]` (snippet).
- **No explicit statewide site-identification or suitability-scoring recommendation** was found on the summary page. The full PDF was not read, so this is an absence in the summary, not in the plan.

## PHFA

- **PHARE 2026:** over **$95M**, with priority on preserving existing rental stock or creating new affordable rental `[skimmed]` ([PHFA Act 105 page](https://www.phfa.org/legislation/act105.aspx)).
- **2026 housing policy fellowship:** a 2026 PHFA housing policy fellow is researching **Pittsburgh and Wilkinsburg's "over 27,000 vacant lots… many publicly owned and irregularly shaped."** `[read]` ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/phillips-sing-named-phfa-2026-housing-policy-fellows-302639497.html)). The stakeholder sweep judged this the closest match to the Track 1 concept among the stakeholders it found, and a natural person to validate with.
- **Financing rules that feed a pro forma** `[read]` (from the prior-art sweep; detail belongs in [pro forma](../methods/pro-forma.md)):
  - 2025/2026 QAP maximum basis per unit: **$320,000 (9% credits)** and **$380,000 (4% with tax-exempt bonds)**, excluding developer fee and acquisition ([QAP PDF](https://www.phfa.org/forms/multifamily_program_notices/qap/2025_and_2026/2025-2026-lihtc-qap.pdf)).
  - Development Cost Limits (revised 1/17/25): construction contingency 5% new / 10% rehab, among others ([PHFA MAI-07](https://www.phfa.org/forms/multifamily_application_guidelines/application/2025-2026-mai-07.pdf)).
  - Keystone homebuyer income limit for Allegheny County (July 2026): $110,400 (1–2 persons), $126,900 (3+) `[skimmed]` ([PHFA Appendix A](https://www.phfa.org/forms/sellersguide/appendices/a.pdf)).

## What this means for us (inference)

- The state's stated priorities are preservation, repair and regulatory modernization, more than site selection.
- Outputs that count in state applications (e.g. an infrastructure-readiness or site-control evidence package) might be more useful to applicants than a ranking. Not confirmed with PHFA or DCED.
- The "27,000 vacant lots, many irregularly shaped" framing supports treating lot geometry vs. post-reform minimum lot size as a real input ([dimensional standards](../policy/dimensional-standards-and-use-table.md)).

## Open questions

- What does the full Housing Action Plan PDF say about data, site inventories or suitability analysis?
- Which outputs would count in PHARE or LIHTC applications?
- Is the PHFA fellow's vacant-lot work public, and on what data?
- How is the 27,000 figure defined relative to the City's 5,000 tax-delinquent vacant lots and the 45,000 county-wide Lots to Love figure? See [Land Bank](land-bank.md).

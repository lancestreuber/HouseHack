## Assumptions this challenges

1. **"Which site is easiest" is not the constraint anyone names.** The constraints cited are, roughly in order:
   - acquisition and title clearing
   - the financing, subsidy or appraisal gap
   - preservation (losing existing affordable units)
   - inclusionary zoning cost uncertainty
   - zoning and variances
   - Land Bank funding

   Nonprofits usually pursue parcels they already control or that sit in their target neighborhood; they don't shop citywide. A score that ignores acquisition path and time-to-clear-title (tax delinquency, Land Bank status, liens, condemnation) misses what practitioners actually cite.
2. **Permitting is already improving fast; zoning and variances are the remaining regulatory friction**, and a code rewrite is coming. A score hard-coded to today's zoning goes stale. It should support "current code vs. proposed code (2025-1545 / rewrite)" scenarios. That scenario comparison is also useful to policy analysts.
3. **City and County want different things.** The City is growth-first; the County is preservation-first and works through 130 municipalities with model ordinances. A City-only tool fits the City; a multi-municipal zoning-barrier lens fits the County.
4. **Rehab vs. new construction is central, not an edge case.** Nearly all Land Bank structures and the rehab pilot are rehab, and Stadelman warns delay raises rehab cost. But Andrews says rehab often costs more per square foot than new construction. So "ease" for a structure should include condition and the cost of waiting, and probably be a separate track from vacant-lot infill. Vacant lots (5,000 tax-delinquent; 27,000 per Sing) are often irregular or undersized, so lot geometry versus the post-reform minimum lot size is a real input.
5. **"AI" should be modest and auditable.** The only AI use the City has named is completeness checking of applications. Judges scoring data and AI integrity will favor rules-based scoring that cites code sections, with a model used for explanation, over opaque ML.

## Open questions for mentors

1. For the City, URA and Land Bank: would a scored inventory of the tax-delinquent, Land Bank and publicly owned portfolio be more adoptable than scoring arbitrary parcels? Who owns that inventory's data today?
2. Is there an internal data source for acquisition status (treasurer or sheriff sale pipeline, liens, conservatorship) we could use or mock?
3. Which variances most often block small infill — lot size, setbacks, parking, use? Is Zoning Board of Adjustment decision data available in structured form?
4. Should we score against current zoning, Bill 2025-1545 as recommended, or both?
5. Is the City's "AI for missing info" tool procured or a pilot yet? Would our tool conflict with it or complement it?
6. For the County: would a municipality-level "zoning barrier index" help its model-ordinance and incentive work more than a parcel score does?
7. For PHFA and DCED: which outputs would count in PHARE or other state applications, e.g. an infrastructure-readiness or site-control evidence package?
8. Rehab: what condition data exists (condemnation list, code violations), and how should rehab cost risk be weighted against new-build ease?
9. Can we reach Dr. Evaine Sing's PHFA fellowship project on vacant lots?
